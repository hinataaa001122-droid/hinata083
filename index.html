WHAT HROOT CHECKS

One scan, every signal that matters.

HRoot pulls the same signals a technician would check by hand, and puts them in a single readable report you can save or share.

Root manager detection

Identifies Magisk, KernelSU or APatch if any is installed, along with the version in use.

BusyBox presence

Checks whether BusyBox binaries are installed and reachable on the system path.

SELinux status

Reports whether the kernel's security module is Enforcing, Permissive, or Disabled.

Bootloader & A/B slot

Reads bootloader lock state and partition layout (A/B seamless updates or legacy).

Shareable report file

Exports everything into a plain-text report you can keep, send, or attach to a support thread.

Runs without root

The scan itself works on any device — HRoot doesn't request or require root access to check for it.

Permissions & data, in plain terms

HRoot asks for internet access so its ads can load and so the app can check for updates; it does not use that connection to upload your report or device data anywhere. The device information the app reads — model, Android version, CPU architecture, RAM, storage figures, and root-related status — is processed on your device and shown to you directly in the report. HRoot does not require, and does not request, root access to run any of its checks.

Ads in HRoot are served through Google AdMob. Like most ad-supported apps, AdMob may collect advertising identifiers, coarse device information, and interaction data to serve and measure ads, subject to Google's own policies. Where required by local law, you'll see a consent choice for personalized advertising the first time you open the app.

HRoot does not ask you to create an account, does not collect your name, email address, or phone number, and does not knowingly collect information from children. The generated report file stays on your device unless you choose to share it yourself.
